Toothache & Emergency Dental Care in Greater Noida

Fast guidance and treatment for sudden pain, swelling or injury.

About this treatment

Toothache has a habit of arriving at the worst moment. The pain can come from decay, a cracked tooth, an infected nerve, an abscess, sore gums or an erupting wisdom tooth. The right treatment depends on the cause, so the first step is to find out what is really going on, not just to cover the pain.

When you may need it

  • Sharp or throbbing pain that does not settle
  • Swelling of the gum, cheek or face
  • A broken, chipped or knocked-out tooth
  • Pain that wakes you at night
  • A bad taste in the mouth with tooth pain
  • Bleeding that does not stop after an injury

How treatment works

  1. Call usTell us what is happening. We will guide you on what to do and how soon to come in.
  2. Quick assessmentWe examine the area and, if needed, take an X-ray to identify the source of the pain.
  3. Relief firstWhere possible we relieve the pain and control infection at the very first visit.
  4. Treatment planWe explain the permanent treatment needed, such as a filling, root canal or extraction, with the estimate.
  5. Follow-upWe check your recovery and complete any remaining treatment.
What it feels like. If you are in pain, tell us right away; there is no need to wait until you feel brave. Our aim at an emergency visit is to calm the problem down first and plan the rest together.

Aftercare and tips

  • Rinse gently with warm salt water
  • Use a cold compress on the cheek for swelling
  • Take over-the-counter pain relief only as the label directs, and never place tablets directly on the gum
  • Avoid very hot, cold or hard food on the painful side
  • If a tooth is knocked out, hold it by the crown (not the root), rinse gently, keep it moist in milk and see a dentist as quickly as possible
  • Swelling that spreads to the face or neck, fever, or trouble swallowing or breathing needs urgent medical care; go to a hospital emergency department

Common causes of toothache

Toothache has many causes, and the right treatment depends on which one it is. Deep decay that has reached the nerve, a cracked tooth, an abscess at the root, an inflamed or infected gum, a loose filling, sensitivity from worn enamel and an erupting wisdom tooth are among the most frequent. A proper examination and often an X-ray are needed to tell them apart, which is why self-medicating for days is rarely a good idea.

What to do while you wait for your appointment

Rinse gently with warm salt water, floss softly in case food is lodged, and use a cold pack on the cheek for swelling. Over-the-counter pain relief can be used as directed on its label. Please do not place tablets directly on the gum, and do not try to burst a swelling yourself. Keep your head slightly raised when resting, since lying flat can make throbbing worse.

Dental emergencies that need quick action

A knocked-out tooth, a badly broken tooth, heavy bleeding after an injury and swelling that is spreading all call for urgent attention. If a permanent tooth is knocked out, pick it up by the crown rather than the root, rinse it gently without scrubbing, keep it moist in milk and get to a dentist right away; the sooner it is replaced, the better its chances. If you have swelling of the face or neck, fever, or trouble swallowing or breathing, go to a hospital emergency department.

Treatment for tooth pain at Pranjal Dental Clinic

Our first aim is to settle the pain and control any infection. Depending on the cause, that may mean cleaning and filling a cavity, starting a root canal, repairing a fracture, treating the gum or removing a tooth that cannot be saved. We always explain the options and the estimated cost before proceeding, and we schedule the rest of the treatment at a time that suits you.

Why early treatment matters

A small cavity treated early is quick and economical. The same tooth left for months can need a root canal, a crown or even extraction. If a toothache has gone quiet, the problem has not necessarily gone away; sometimes the nerve has died while the infection continues beneath. If you have had tooth pain, even if it has settled, please book a check-up.
